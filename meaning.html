Meaning

Does khaana mean "food," or "to eat"?

Both, depending on how it's used. Khaana is a noun meaning "food" or "a meal," and it's also the infinitive form of the verb "to eat." Hindi grammar is why the same word does both jobs.

As a noun: food, a meal

Used as a noun, खाना (khaana) simply means "food" or "a meal," in the general sense, not any specific dish.

Noun
खाना तैयार है।
Khaana taiyār hai.
The food is ready.
Noun
मुझे घर का खाना पसंद है।
Mujhe ghar kā khaana pasand hai.
I like home-cooked food.

As a verb: to eat

खाना (khaana) is also the infinitive form of the Hindi verb "to eat." Hindi infinitives regularly end in -nā (करना/karnā "to do," जाना/jānā "to go," खाना/khaana "to eat"), so this isn't a coincidence of two unrelated words looking alike. It's the same word doing what Hindi infinitives do.

Verb
मैं खाना खा रहा हूँ।
Maiṅ khaana khā rahā hūṅ.
I am eating (food).
Verb
क्या तुमने खाना खाया?
Kyā tumne khaana khāyā?
Did you eat?
Why one word covers both: the sentence "मैं खाना खा रहा हूँ" (Maiṅ khaana khā rahā hūṅ) literally uses खाना twice, once as the noun object ("food") and once inside the conjugated verb ("am eating"). That's not repetition for effect; it's just how the grammar works, and it's a good example of how the noun and verb senses sit side by side in everyday speech.

Context does the work

In practice, Hindi and Urdu speakers rarely find this ambiguous. "खाना बनाना" (khaana banānā) means "to cook" (literally, "to make food"). "खाना खाना" (khaana khaana) means "to eat" (literally, "to eat food," using both senses back to back). Sentence position and the surrounding grammar make the intended meaning clear, the same way "watch" can be a noun or a verb in English without causing confusion.
