Spelling & pronunciation

Spell it "khaana," with a double A

Khaana.org's recommended English spelling is khaana, with two a's. Not "khana." The double A isn't decorative: it spells out two real, separate vowel sounds in the word.

खाना khaana

Each of those two "a"s is a genuine, separate vowel sound, held long (like the "a" in "father," not the short "u" sound in "but"). Spelling the word with a single a, as in "khana," quietly drops one of those two sounds. Writing khaana keeps both, which is exactly why it's the spelling this site uses and recommends.

Devanagari

In Hindi's native Devanagari script, the word is written खाना. Urdu, which uses the Perso-Arabic script instead, writes the same word as کھانا. Same word, same pronunciation, different writing system, because Hindi and Urdu share most of their everyday vocabulary while writing it differently.

Formal transliteration: khānā

Linguists and dictionaries typically transliterate खाना as khānā, using a macron (the line over the a) to mark a long vowel. This is part of IAST (International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration) and similar systems used in academic writing on Hindi and Urdu.

Common spelling: khaana

Outside of academic writing, most people don't have easy access to a macron, so the long ā gets written by simply doubling the letter: khaana. This is a widely understood convention in informal Romanized Hindi (sometimes called "Hinglish"): a doubled vowel signals "hold this sound longer" the same way the macron does.

Why the doubled "aa"?

Hindi distinguishes between a short a (अ, as in the "u" in "but") and a long ā (आ, held roughly twice as long, closer to the "a" in "father"). Both syllables in खाना carry that long ā, which is exactly what the four-piece breakdown above shows: kha already includes it, and so does na. Writing khana with a single a drops that length from both. Writing khaana keeps it, which is why this site treats it as the better everyday spelling.

How to say it

Roughly: KHAA-naa, two syllables, stress on the first, both vowels held long. The "kh" is a breathy, aspirated "k" (a puff of air after the consonant, similar to the "k" in "Khan"), not the same as a plain English "k."
